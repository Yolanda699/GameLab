Maya pipeline highlights

  • 76 asset scenes in Maya: 23 dolls, 16 buildings, 12 flats, 24 props and the ride boat
  • One doll kit: 23 costumes on a shared body, all bound to the same 13-joint skeleton
  • 10 tileable texture sets (BC / N / R) tinted into 85 Maya materials
  • Every Maya material named after its Unreal instance: 126 MIs off one master
  • IK/FK arms with pole vectors, colour-coded controls, max 4 influences per vertex
  • 17 keyframed clips on referenced rigs: 13 shared doll clips and 4 prop clips

01Overview

Little World is a psychological-horror boat ride inspired by It’s a Small World at Hong Kong Disneyland. The player travels through a brightly coloured showroom of singing dolls in costumes from across Asia. We want the cheerful setting and synchronised movement to become unsettling as the ride continues.

This is a two-person USC CTIN 534 project in Unreal Engine 5.8. I handle art and technical art; Katrina Xiao handles level design, systems and engineering, including the ride path, boat behaviour and music zones. This page documents the current Maya modelling, material setup, rigging and animation, followed by Unreal integration. Lighting, VFX, sound and UI are planned for later passes.

The pipeline at a glance

The assets follow a shared workflow from Maya to Unreal. Consistent scene structure and naming help me manage the kit and update it during production.

Model

Maya · one scene per asset

Real-world scale in cm, pivot at the base, history deleted, transforms frozen, named SM_ / SK_.

Texture

Shared library

10 tileable sets, tinted per material; Maya shader names match the Unreal instances.

Rig

One skeleton for 23 dolls

13 bind joints with engine-friendly names, IK/FK arms, separate control and joint layers.

Animate

Referenced rig

Clips keyed on controls in their own scenes, so clips can load updates from the shared rig.

Integrate

Unreal 5.8

FBX into a folder tree that mirrors Maya; one skeleton, one master material.

02Modelling

The doll kit

I built the 23 dolls around a shared body, with an oversized head, short torso, small hands and stub shoes designed to read from the moving boat. Costumes, hair and headwear distinguish each character. Named parts such as Head_GEO, Body_GEO and Sleeve_l_GEO have separate materials and skin clusters.

Clay and wireframe views of the same turnaround. The mesh is predominantly quads, with evenly spaced edges around the sleeves and shoulders for skinning.

Modelled facial features

The eyes, brows, cheeks, nose and mouth are separate shapes with their own materials. This avoids a dedicated facial texture and lets me adjust the expression by editing geometry. Facial animation is a possible later addition.

Heads

About 7.8k faces on a sphere-based grid; the same head on every doll.

Dolls

44k to 74k triangles each in Maya, 26k to 46k vertices in Unreal.

Parts

8 to 12 meshes per doll, one material slot each, so every costume colour is a material swap.

Set pieces

The boat passes a torii gate, Korean gate, Chinese pagoda, Thai prang and chedi, minaret and Taj Mahal-style palace. I built these as stylised stage flats and dioramas, using simple shapes and concentrating detail on the sides visible from the canal.

Around them sit 24 props and 12 flats: lanterns, lotus flowers, fans, pinwheels, clouds, a dragon, topiary, lamp posts and the ride boat itself.

Scene conventions

Each asset has its own scene, using shared naming, scale and hierarchy conventions to simplify export and placement.

Naming

SM_ for static meshes, SK_ for skinned ones, A_ for animation scenes, then area and asset: SM_Asia_KoreanGate, SK_Doll_Samurai, A_Doll_FanDance.

Folders

scenes/Asia/Architecture, Asia/Flats, Asia/Props, Characters/Doll, Queue/…, Boat/…: the same tree the assets land in inside Unreal.

Scale & pivot

Centimetres, Y-up, the asset sitting on the origin with its pivot at the base, so it snaps to the floor when placed in the level.

Hierarchy

Static assets: one top node with named meshes. Skinned assets: SKEL, GEO and RIG groups under the asset node.

03Texturing

To reuse textures across the kit, I built the materials around ten tileable texture sets. Each contains base colour, tangent-space normal and roughness maps at 1024². Near-neutral sets can be tinted for different costumes: the same Fabric set is used for a red kimono, navy samurai coat and pastel hanbok.

One material model in both tools

Maya materials share a Standard Surface setup and use the same names as their Unreal instances. For example, MI_LW_Doll_Red identifies the material in both tools. Matching the 85 Maya material names to existing Unreal instances helps keep reimports organised.

Consistent texel density

I kept the doll UVs at a consistent world scale so fabric patterns would appear similar in size across the head, hair, sleeves and skirt. The UVs extend beyond 0–1 to support the tileable maps; the checker view shows their relative scale.

I adjust pattern scale per material with Tiling and use the corresponding setting in Unreal.

04Rigging

All 23 dolls use the same 13-joint skeleton layout, allowing them to share animations. It includes the root, pelvis, spine, neck, head and both arm chains, with consistent joint names for Unreal. The intended engine setup uses one shared Skeleton asset; consolidating leftover imports is still on the clean-up list.

Skeleton13 bind jointsA short spine (pelvis, spine, neck, head) and full arm chains; no legs, because the dolls glide on their pedestals
ArmsIK / FKSeparate FK_ and IK_ chains blended onto the bind arm, pole vector controls, one ikFk switch per side on arm_*_settings_CTRL
Controls20 curvesglobal, root, cog, spine, neck, head, clavicles and arms, each on an offset group so they zero cleanly
SkinningPer-part clustersOne skin cluster per mesh part, max 4 influences per vertex for real-time
LayersGEO · JNT · CTRLMesh layer referenced, joints hidden, controls on their own layer: an animator can only grab what they should

Rigged props

The same approach runs through the moving set pieces, each with a small purpose-built skeleton:

SK_Asia_Kite

6 joints: a root, the kite body and a four-joint tail, so the tail follows through behind the swing.

SK_Asia_Parasol

2 joints: shaft and canopy, so the canopy can spin independently while a doll holds the shaft.

SK_Queue_ClockTower

6 joints: hour and minute hands, the pendulum and two doors that open for the parade.

05Animation

Each clip lives in its own scene (A_Doll_Wave.ma, A_Doll_FanDance.ma…) that references the rig under a rig: namespace instead of copying it. Keys sit on the controls only. If the rig changes, every clip picks it up on the next open, and because every doll shares the skeleton, one set of 13 clips animates all 23 dolls.

Ambient

Idle, HeadTilt, Turn, Wave, Bow: the background dolls that line the canal.

Performers

Dance, FanDance, FanFlutter, Parasol, Plates: the featured dolls on stages and pedestals.

Musicians

Drum, Cymbal, HoldSing: the band and the choir, kept on simple, readable beats.

07Status & next

Work in progress. The Maya kit above is in the engine and playing; the screenshots are editor captures from the current build, not final lighting.

Challenge

23 dolls, one artist

Building separate bodies, rigs and animation libraries for every doll would add maintenance work and make the cast harder to keep consistent.

Solution

Shared doll components

Shared body and proportions, one skeleton, one clip library. A new doll is a new costume, skinned to a rig that already works.

Challenge

Material consistency

Unique texture sets for every asset would increase memory use and the amount of texture work.

Solution

Ten tileable sets and a shared material model

Tint and tiling are parameters, set once in Maya and mirrored on one Unreal master, with the same names on both sides.

Challenge

Rig changes after animation started

Separate copies of the rig would need to be updated across all 17 animation scenes.

Solution

Referenced animation scenes

Clips reference the rig file and key only controls, so updated rigs can be loaded in each animation scene before checking and re-exporting the clips.

Next passes

Lighting

A lighting pass per scene of the ride, then the shift from cheerful to uneasy as the boat goes deeper.

VFX & sound

Lantern glow, water and the music cues that the dolls perform to.

Animation

Add variation and timing offsets between dolls, reserving synchronised movement for specific moments in the ride.

Clean-up

Consolidate leftover per-import skeleton assets onto SKEL_Doll and tidy duplicate animation imports.
