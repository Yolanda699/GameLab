Maya pipeline highlights

  • 76 asset scenes in Maya: 23 dolls, 16 buildings, 12 flats, 24 props and the ride boat
  • One doll kit: 23 costumes on a shared body, all bound to the same 13-joint skeleton
  • 10 tileable texture sets (BC / N / R) tinted into 85 Maya materials
  • Every Maya material named after its Unreal instance: 126 MIs off one master
  • IK/FK arms with pole vectors, colour-coded controls, max 4 influences per vertex
  • 17 keyframed clips on referenced rigs, playing on all 23 dolls in Unreal

01Overview

Little World is a short psychological-horror ride inspired by It's a Small World at Hong Kong Disneyland. You queue under a clock tower, step into a boat and drift down a canal through a showroom of singing dolls in costumes from across Asia. Everything is cheerful, bright and perfectly in time, which is exactly what should start to feel wrong. We are aiming for unease rather than jump scares.

It is a two-person USC CTIN 534 project in Unreal Engine 5.8. I am the artist and technical artist; Katrina Xiao owns level design, systems and engineering (the ride path, the boat and the music zones). The game is still in production, so for now this page covers only my Maya work: how the dolls and the set were modelled, textured, rigged and animated, and how they get into the engine. Lighting, VFX, sound and UI will follow once those passes are done.

The pipeline at a glance

Every asset moves through the same five stages, and every stage leaves the asset in a state the next one can rely on. That is what lets one artist keep 76 assets consistent.

Model

Maya · one scene per asset

Real-world scale in cm, pivot at the base, history deleted, transforms frozen, named SM_ / SK_.

Texture

Shared library

10 tileable sets, tinted per material; Maya shader names match the Unreal instances.

Rig

One skeleton for 23 dolls

13 bind joints with engine-friendly names, IK/FK arms, separate control and joint layers.

Animate

Referenced rig

Clips keyed on controls in their own scenes, so a rig fix reaches every clip.

Integrate

Unreal 5.8

FBX into a folder tree that mirrors Maya; one skeleton, one master material.

02Modelling

The doll kit

The dolls are the heart of the ride, and there are a lot of them, so I built them as a kit rather than 23 one-offs. Every doll shares the same proportions: an oversized round head, a short body, small hands and stub shoes, read clearly from a moving boat. Costume, hair and headwear change per doll. Each doll is split into named parts (Head_GEO, Hair_GEO, Body_GEO, Arm_l_GEO, Sleeve_l_GEO, Hand_l_GEO, Shoes_GEO, plus hats and hand props) so each part can carry its own material and be skinned on its own.

Clay and wireframe of the same turnaround: a quad-based, evenly spaced grid (95% quads, triangles only at the poles), so sleeves and shoulders bend cleanly once skinned.

The face is geometry, not a texture

Eyes, brows, cheeks, nose and mouth are modelled as separate shapes sitting on the head, each with its own material. That keeps the face razor-sharp at any distance with no texture memory, lets me restyle an expression by moving vertices, and means the face can be animated later without repainting anything.

Heads

About 7.8k faces on a sphere-based grid; the same head on every doll.

Dolls

44k to 74k triangles each in Maya, 26k to 46k vertices in Unreal.

Parts

8 to 12 meshes per doll, one material slot each, so every costume colour is a material swap.

Set pieces

The showroom is a run of landmarks the boat passes: a torii gate, a Korean gate, a Chinese pagoda, a Thai prang and chedi, a minaret and a Taj Mahal-style palace. Like a real dark ride, they are stylised stage flats and dioramas, not architecture: simple primitives with strong profiles, readable from one side of the canal, with the detail spent where the camera sees it.

Around them sit 24 props and 12 flats: lanterns, lotus flowers, fans, pinwheels, clouds, a dragon, topiary, lamp posts and the ride boat itself.

Scene conventions

One asset per scene, and every scene opens the same way. This is what makes 76 files exportable without opening each one to check it.

Naming

SM_ for static meshes, SK_ for skinned ones, A_ for animation scenes, then area and asset: SM_Asia_KoreanGate, SK_Doll_Samurai, A_Doll_FanDance.

Folders

scenes/Asia/Architecture, Asia/Flats, Asia/Props, Characters/Doll, Queue/…, Boat/…: the same tree the assets land in inside Unreal.

Scale & pivot

Centimetres, Y-up, the asset sitting on the origin with its pivot at the base, so it snaps to the floor when placed in the level.

Hierarchy

Static assets: one top node with named meshes. Skinned assets: SKEL, GEO and RIG groups under the asset node.

03Texturing

With 76 assets and one artist, unique texture sets per asset were never an option. Instead the whole ride shares one library of ten tileable texture sets, each with a base colour, a tangent-space normal and a roughness map at 1024². Most are near-neutral, so one set can become many materials by tinting: the same Fabric set is a red kimono, a navy samurai coat and a pastel hanbok.

One material model in both tools

Every Maya material is wired the same way and named after the Unreal instance it becomes (MI_LW_Doll_Red in Maya is MI_LW_Doll_Red in the Content Browser). All 85 material names used across the Maya scenes exist as instances in Unreal, so a re-import never creates a stray material and a look tweak happens in exactly one place.

Consistent texel density

Tileable textures only work if every surface is UV'd at the same scale; otherwise the weave on a sleeve is twice the size of the weave on the body. The doll UVs are laid out in world-proportional space rather than squeezed into 0–1, so a checker lands the same size on head, hair, sleeves and skirt.

Pattern scale is then a single number per material (Tiling), not a UV edit per mesh, and it carries straight across to Unreal.

04Rigging

23 dolls that all need to wave, bow and dance would be 23 rigs to maintain. They are one. Every doll is bound to the same 13-joint skeleton, with joint names chosen for the engine (root, pelvis, spine, neck, head, clavicle_l, upperarm_l, lowerarm_l, hand_l and the right side), so in Unreal they share one Skeleton asset and every clip plays on every doll.

Skeleton13 bind jointsA short spine (pelvis, spine, neck, head) and full arm chains; no legs, because the dolls glide on their pedestals
ArmsIK / FKSeparate FK_ and IK_ chains blended onto the bind arm, pole vector controls, one ikFk switch per side on arm_*_settings_CTRL
Controls20 curvesglobal, root, cog, spine, neck, head, clavicles and arms, each on an offset group so they zero cleanly
SkinningPer-part clustersOne skin cluster per mesh part, max 4 influences per vertex for real-time
LayersGEO · JNT · CTRLMesh layer referenced, joints hidden, controls on their own layer: an animator can only grab what they should

Rigged props

The same approach runs through the moving set pieces, each with a small purpose-built skeleton:

SK_Asia_Kite

6 joints: a root, the kite body and a four-joint tail, so the tail follows through behind the swing.

SK_Asia_Parasol

2 joints: shaft and canopy, so the canopy can spin independently while a doll holds the shaft.

SK_Queue_ClockTower

6 joints: hour and minute hands, the pendulum and two doors that open for the parade.

05Animation

Each clip lives in its own scene (A_Doll_Wave.ma, A_Doll_FanDance.ma…) that references the rig under a rig: namespace instead of copying it. Keys sit on the controls only. If the rig changes, every clip picks it up on the next open, and because every doll shares the skeleton, one set of 13 clips animates all 23 dolls.

Ambient

Idle, HeadTilt, Turn, Wave, Bow: the background dolls that line the canal.

Performers

Dance, FanDance, FanFlutter, Parasol, Plates: the featured dolls on stages and pedestals.

Musicians

Drum, Cymbal, HoldSing: the band and the choir, kept on simple, readable beats.

07Status & next

Work in progress. The Maya kit above is in the engine and playing; the screenshots are editor captures from the current build, not final lighting.

Challenge

23 dolls, one artist

A unique model, rig and animation set per doll would never fit a course schedule, and they would drift apart in style.

Solution

Build a kit, not a cast

Shared body and proportions, one skeleton, one clip library. A new doll is a new costume, skinned to a rig that already works.

Challenge

Hundreds of surfaces, one look

Per-asset textures would cost memory and make every colour change a repaint.

Solution

Ten tileable sets and a shared material model

Tint and tiling are parameters, set once in Maya and mirrored on one Unreal master, with the same names on both sides.

Challenge

Rig changes after animation started

Copying a rig into every clip scene means fixing it 17 times.

Solution

Reference, don't copy

Clips reference the rig file and key only controls, so a rig update flows into every clip and every re-export.

Next passes

Lighting

A lighting pass per scene of the ride, then the shift from cheerful to uneasy as the boat goes deeper.

VFX & sound

Lantern glow, water and the music cues that the dolls perform to.

Animation

More variation per doll, offsets so the crowd never moves in perfect unison (until it should).

Clean-up

Consolidate leftover per-import skeleton assets onto SKEL_Doll and tidy duplicate animation imports.
